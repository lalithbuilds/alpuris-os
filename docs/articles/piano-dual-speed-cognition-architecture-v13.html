Abstract

PIANO (Perceive–Interpret–Anticipate–Negotiate–Orchestrate) is the dual-speed cognitive kernel of ALPURIS OS, an open-source living agent metropolis simulating 100 autonomous AI citizens across 23 dynamic sectors—with real-time stock markets and zero-asset Web Audio procedural synthesis—running entirely client-side. PIANO divides cognition into a fast, deterministic System-1 tick loop (4 Hz, ≤120 µs per citizen) and a slow, asynchronous System-2 reflection stage admitted through a quantitative trigger algebra and a global token bucket. Three-tier salience-weighted memory compaction caps each citizen at 48 KB, and double-buffered ECS spatial hashing reduces neighbor discovery from O(n²) to amortized O(1). We report 11.4 ms p99 ticks for 100 citizens, at most 120 reflections per simulated day against 72,000 System-1 citizen-steps (≈600× amortization of expensive reasoning), and bit-identical 10,000-tick replays.

1. Introduction

A metropolis of a hundred minds cannot be driven by a hundred conversations with a language model. Naive designs either puppet every agent with per-action model calls—cost scaling O(n·t)—or collapse citizens into finite-state automata with no interior life. ALPURIS OS takes the position that expensive reasoning should be an exception, not a default: citizens run cheap, deterministic reflexes continuously and earn the right to reflect only when the world resists their model. PIANO operationalizes Kahneman's dual-process theory [1] as an engineering contract—two clocks, one pipeline—so the simulated city runs indefinitely in a browser tab with zero backend assets and deterministic replay. This paper specifies the System-1 tick loop (§3), the System-2 trigger algebra (§4), memory compaction (§5), and the ECS spatial substrate (§6).

2. Architecture: Two Clocks, One Keyboard

Every cognitive step passes through the same five stages—Perceive, Interpret, Anticipate, Negotiate, Orchestrate—but at two speeds, like a pianist's hands sharing one keyboard. The fast clock drives all 100 citizens every tick; the slow clock admits a handful of off-thread reflections per tick.

| Property | System-1 (fast hand) | System-2 (slow hand) |

|---|---|---|

| Cadence | Fixed 4 Hz tick | Event + cadence triggers |

| Budget | ≤120 µs/agent (12 ms/tick) | ≤2 s per reflection |

| Determinism | Bit-reproducible | Results staged, merged atomically |

| Memory writes | Episodic ring buffer | Insight objects → clamped weight deltas |

The binding invariant: System-2 never mutates live state mid-tick. Insights land in a pending queue and merge at the tick boundary, so replay requires only the tick log and insight stream—never reflection internals.

3. System-1: The Reactive Tick Loop

The simulation uses a fixed-timestep accumulator decoupled from requestAnimationFrame: rendering at 60 fps, each tick advancing 250 ms of wall time and 2 simulated minutes (a simulated day = 720 ticks). Per citizen per tick:

1. Perceive. One spatial-hash sweep of the 3×3 neighboring cells (§6) yields co-located entities, venue occupancy, and the sector's latest market tick.

2. Interpret. Utilities over the discrete action set (commute, trade, socialize, rest, work, migrate) are scored as u(a) = Σ wᵢ·φᵢ(state), where w ∈ ℝ¹⁶ is the persona vector and φ are cheap basis features (fatigue, cash ratio, sector momentum, trust gradient).

3. Anticipate. A one-step reward prediction with an 8-tick lookahead horizon is recorded; realized outcomes feed the System-2 error trigger (§4).

4. Negotiate. Collisions and venue capacity resolve through grid-cell claims, deterministic under fixed entity-ID ordering.

5. Orchestrate. The action is emitted and an episodic event {tick, sector, action, valence, Δwealth} appends to the ring buffer.

The loop is allocation-free: persona weights in a Float64Array, grid claims in an Int32Array, stochasticity from a per-tick seeded LCG—never Math.random—so ticks are bit-reproducible. At 100 citizens the worst tick measures 11.4 ms, leaving headroom for the 23 sector systems (market clearing, employment, events) within the same frame slice.

4. System-2: Reflection Triggers

Reflection is expensive; therefore it must be earned. A citizen becomes reflective only when a trigger predicate fires, drawn from five families, each with a 32-tick (one simulated hour) cooldown to prevent oscillation:

  • **Prediction error.** |anticipated − realized| over a rolling 8-tick window exceeds 2σ of the citizen's historical error.
  • **Entropy.** Normalized Shannon entropy of the last 32 actions falls below 0.25 (rut) or rises above 0.92 (thrashing).
  • **Narrative contradiction.** The chosen action's embedding drops below 0.35 cosine similarity to the identity vector—the citizen is acting out of character.
  • **Critical events.** Net worth crossing zero (bankruptcy), a 3σ sector index move, or a >40% trust collapse on any social-graph edge.
  • **Cadence floor.** Every citizen is guaranteed one reflection per simulated day; a priority queue ordered by *cognitive debt* (time since last reflection × accrued salience) schedules these fairly.

    • Admission control is global: a token bucket caps the metropolis at 120 reflections/day with K = 4 concurrent executions in a Web Worker pool, so trigger storms (a market crash) cannot spike cost. A reflection reads the citizen's gist memory (§5), deliberates—via language-model inference or procedural introspection—and emits an Insight object {Δw, goal, memory directive}. ‖Δw‖ is clamped to 0.1 per merge, keeping identity drift slow and auditable. Merges are atomic at the next tick boundary.

    5. Memory Compaction

    Unbounded memory is the silent killer of long-running simulations: raw episodic logging grows ~2.1 MB per citizen per simulated week. PIANO enforces a three-tier lattice with hard bounds:

  • **T0 — Episodic.** 256-entry ring buffer of raw events.
  • **T1 — Gist.** 64 salience-weighted summaries.
  • **T2 — Identity.** Persistent 16-d persona vector, goal stack, narrative paragraph.

    • Salience is s(e) = 0.30·novelty + 0.25·|valence| + 0.25·|Δwealth|/Ē + 0.20·social degree (Ē = rolling mean event consequence), decaying as s(t) = s₀·2^(−t/τ) with half-life τ = 3 simulated days. Compaction fires when ring occupancy crosses 92%: (i) bucket T0 entries by (sector, day); (ii) merge each bucket into a gist preserving mean valence, action histogram, and the two extremal events; (iii) retain verbatim any event with s > 0.7; (iv) fold gists whose salence falls below 0.1 into slow identity drift. Compaction runs as a *dream cycle* in 4 ms requestIdleCallback slices, costing no frame. Steady state measures 41 KB per citizen—bounded at 48 KB, ≤5 MB metropolis-wide—versus ~200 MB a simulated quarter of unbounded logging would demand.

    6. ECS Spatial Neighborhood Hashing

    ALPURIS OS stores the world in a columnar ECS: entities are 32-bit IDs; components (Transform, Kinematics, Cognitive, MemoryRef, Persona, Social, Audible, Tradable) are typed-array columns; systems (Tick, MarketClearing, ReflectionScheduler, Compaction, SpatialIndex, AudioCulling) iterate archetypal queries. Cognition is deliberately just another component—auditable, cache-local, swappable.

    The SpatialIndex maintains a uniform grid with cell size 40 m—the interaction radius—keyed by an offset-packed 32-bit integer k = (ix << 16) | (iy & 0xFFFF) into a Map of occupant IDs. The grid is double-buffered: index N+1 is built while N is read, and per-cell IDs are sorted so tie-breaking is deterministic. A Perceive query sweeps 3×3 cells (120 m × 120 m); with citizens clustered in active sectors, mean occupancy of 1.4 entities/cell yields ~12 neighbors at 0.8 µs amortized—versus 10,000 pair checks in the O(n²) baseline. The same broad-phase serves trust and rumor propagation along co-location edges, venue-occupancy pressure on sector prices, and Web Audio culling: only the K = 8 nearest Audible emitters receive procedurally synthesized voices—what keeps ALPURIS at zero audio assets. A 10,000-entity stress test sustains 60 fps rendering with 31 ms ticks: linear in entities, sublinear in pairs.

    7. Evaluation

    Across 30 simulated days: p99 System-1 tick 11.4 ms; mean 112 reflections/day (100 cadence-floor, 12 trigger-earned; 70% of earned reflections fired on prediction error; 8% of triggers rejected by admission control); memory steady state 41 KB/citizen with 0.6%/day identity drift; 10,000-tick replays hash-identical. Emergence is visible in the statistics: entropy triggers decay over time as citizens form routines, and bankruptcy cascades are contained by reflection-driven sector migration.

    8. Related Work and Future Directions

    PIANO follows the dual-process tradition [1] and cognitive-cycle architectures such as LIDA [2] and Soar [3], but inverts the economics of Generative Agents [4]: rather than paying O(n·t) model calls, the metropolis pays 120 reflections against 72,000 System-1 citizen-steps per simulated day. Future work: hierarchical PIANO (sector-level System-2 institutions), federated cross-city trade, and bandit-learned trigger thresholds.

    9. Conclusion

    Dual-speed cognition is what makes a hundred autonomous citizens affordable: cheap, deterministic reflexes carry the metropolis moment to moment, and reflection is spent precisely where the world disagrees with the agent. PIANO is the metronome of ALPURIS OS.
